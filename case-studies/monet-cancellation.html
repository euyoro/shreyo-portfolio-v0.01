Context

What do you show a user who's trying to leave?

Monet is Bright's monetization surface. Every screen a user touches when they subscribe, manage their plan, or cancel. I've been part of this project across phases. The cancellation flow was mine to own.

The Problem

The Starter tier

Bright ran two tiers: Premium or nothing. A user who cancelled went to $0. There was no middle.

Premium $11 / mo

Full subscription. All features unlocked.

Starter — New $5 / mo

Cash advance, credit features, Rent Reporting as add-on. A way to stay in the product.

Basic $0

Cancelled. Limited access.

Two user groups needed designing for: existing users being migrated to Starter automatically with a 30-day grace period, and Premium users actively cancelling who needed to see the $5 offer.

Decision

When to show the offer

Netflix shows you a cheaper plan before you cancel. Audible does the same. The mid-flow downsell is a proven pattern — but only works if the offer is genuinely strong.

I designed multiple flow variants. Variant 1 had stronger visuals. Variant 2 put the $5 offer at the right moment in the flow. We aligned on Variant 2 for the first test. Not because it looked better on screen. Because it gave users the information at the moment it was actually useful.

Fallback
Post-cancellation offer
Confirm the cancel, then pitch Starter. Designed and ready. Some users respond better when the product honours their decision first.

Constraints

The compliance layer

Every screen in Monet touches compliance. Cancellation screens even more so.

Two flags from the legal review. The word "free" appeared on a final screen where Starter costs $5 a month. It came out. The phrase "you are eligible" appeared above a loan offer, implying an approval the user didn't have. Rewritten to match compliance-reviewed copy on the Bright website exactly.

These aren't copy notes. Getting them wrong on a cancellation screen is a legal exposure.

Complexity

The state machine

The hardest part wasn't the cancellation flow. It was the number of states a single user could be in.

Migrated users in notice period. Migrated in grace period. Migrated once billing started. Premium cancelled. Starter force opt-in. Starter explicit opt-in. Basic. Each with different copy, different CTAs, different settings screens. Eight distinct states. I mapped every one and designed them all.

Outcome

What shipped

Premium cancellation flow: closed and signed off. Starter cancellation flow: feedback implemented, final review pending at handoff. The full settings states and wireframe set were delivered and with engineering.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

  • Map all user states before touching the design. Eight states across three user types is a known complexity for a tiered subscription product. I discovered the full scope through iteration instead of up front.
  • The mid-flow vs. post-cancellation decision needed data I didn't have. I'd push for prior A/B test results from a comparable product before locking direction. We chose right based on reasoning. Data would have made it faster.
