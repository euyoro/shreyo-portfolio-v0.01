Context

How can a book build meaning?

GLS Institute of Design lacked a dedicated space, physical or digital, to showcase student and alumni achievements annually. There was no cohesive platform to document patents, publications, awards, or the creative work produced across disciplines each year. That absence was the brief.

The project was to create an engaging and visually compelling annual yearbook and digital archive platform to capture, celebrate, document, and showcase the creative journey and accomplishments of GLS Institute of Design throughout the year. The book needed to serve as both a keepsake and a repository, a record that students and faculty could refer back to.

Approach

Building a visual language for the institute

The visual language was built to be minimal, concise, and clean. The design system needed to stay quiet enough that the students' own work could be the loudest thing on every spread.

Typography and Color

Avenir Next LT Pro was chosen as the primary typeface for its neutrality and legibility at small sizes. The color scheme was deliberately neutral: black, white, and warm grays for the structure, with vibrant colored panels reserved for the students' work. Brackets [] were used throughout as a recurring motif, assigning symbolic meaning to each discipline category.

Typography & Color System

Grid System

The layout alternated between a column grid for text-heavy pages and a modular grid for portfolio spreads. This gave the book a consistent rhythm while accommodating the variety of student work, from photography to product design to illustration.

Grid System & Spread Layouts

AR Integration

An augmented reality layer was built using Artvive, allowing readers to scan specific pages and access additional content, video walkthroughs, process documentation, and extended project narratives, through their phone. This bridged the physical book with a digital archive.

Print Specifications

The final book was produced at 28 cm x 20 cm, perfect bound, printed on 100 GSM standard paper using offset printing. Every spread was proofed and color-corrected before the final print run.

Outcome

A printed record

The yearbook was printed and distributed within the institute. It documented the full year's creative output across all departments, serving as both a reference and a celebration of the work produced.

"The book felt like a visual journal for all the work done within the institute."
Foundation Student
"The AR introduction felt very new and fascinating to look at!"
Faculty Review

The AR layer gave the book a second life beyond print, allowing viewers to access extended content without adding page count. Studying Josef Muller-Brockmann's "Grid Systems" during the project deepened my understanding of how structure and white space work together to give content room to breathe.

Reflection

What I took away

  • Art direction for print requires a different kind of restraint than screen design. The book's job was to frame the students' work, not compete with it. Neutral typography and a quiet color system made the vibrant portfolio pieces feel intentional, not chaotic.
  • Grid discipline is what makes a 100-page book feel like one object instead of a collection of unrelated spreads. The column-to-modular grid switch gave the layout flexibility without breaking consistency.
  • AR integration added a digital layer without complicating the physical experience. The technology was invisible until the reader chose to engage with it.
  • Managing art direction, content editing, and print production simultaneously built a working understanding of how decisions in one domain cascade into the others. A type change on page 12 can break the grid on page 40.
