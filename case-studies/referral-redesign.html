Context

Who tells a friend about an app they barely use?

That was the real question. Not how to clean up the flow. Whether anyone actually wanted to refer Bright, and whether the screen helped them do it.

Bright Money is a US fintech app. It helps people manage credit card debt, pay bills, save, and build credit. The referral programme is one of the only organic growth levers the product has. A $25 reward for every friend who signs up and completes a qualifying transaction.

I owned this project for 13 months, across three separate phases, each revived when Growth had bandwidth. The brief when it started: Referral 2.0 → 3.0. Nothing else.

Problem

The 2022 UI was doing the asking. Not the selling.

The referral flow had not been touched since 2022. The reward rules had changed. New qualifying actions, a $25 ceiling, a $10 minimum transaction, a five-referral monthly cap with no carry-over. The screens showed none of it.

Three things were broken:

  • The reward structure was buried in copy. Users couldn't skim what they'd actually earn.
  • The PLU — the in-app card surfacing the referral offer — was static. Growth couldn't vary messaging without a dev sprint.
  • The visual system had moved. The old screens were built on a design language the product had since replaced.
Finding 01 Reward ceiling and qualifying conditions were unreadable at a glance
Finding 02 Every PLU messaging test cost Growth a full dev sprint
Finding 03 The 2022 illustration style no longer matched the product

Goals

User

  • Understand the reward without hunting for fine print
  • Get to sharing in one action
  • Trust the offer is worth passing on

Business

  • Higher referral conversion, no backend changes
  • Growth-controlled PLU — testable without a dev sprint
  • Full visual alignment with Phoenix design system

Process

Three phases. Eight months between two of them.

Phase 1 — Aug to Sep 2025

UX from scratch. I mapped the full referral experience in FigJam, built the 3.0 UX flows, explored a scratch-card treatment for the referral card, ideated PLU screens, and locked the illustration direction with Joe.

Phase 2 — Oct to Nov 2025

UI hygiene across all touchpoints. Illustration delivered by Pallavi. Screens ready, aligned with Joe, shared with Ashish. Then: deprioritised. The RMS track took Growth's bandwidth. Screens sat for eight months.

Phase 3 — Jul to Aug 2026

Joe revived it with one constraint: no backend involvement. Wireframes done in a day. Hi-fi closed in a week. Aligned with Ashish and Tanishq. Closed.

Key Decision

The constraint that made Phase 3 faster

Joe's brief on revival: no backend involvement. I took that further than a refresh.

The real ask surfaced in the MoM with Ashish. Growth needed a PLU system they could control dynamically. Vary the message, test the offer, swap the visual. No dev sprint. That meant designing the PLU as a modular component, not a fixed screen.

Ruled out
Static PLU screen
Single message, designed once. Every change needs engineering. Growth can't test without a dev sprint.
Chosen
Modular PLU component
Message, visual, and CTA templated. Growth controls all three from BE + FE. Built to feed the RMS system Ashish was building toward.

The constraint forced the right outcome. A PLU Growth owns is more valuable than one that looks better.

What I questioned

"Eight months, same screens, same result" — mine

Phase 2 closed with Ashish aligned and screens ready. The deprioritisation was a business call, not mine. But I didn't push for a lightweight test. One PLU variant, one sprint, something to keep the work alive through the RMS gap. I waited for full bandwidth. That's how a six-week project becomes thirteen months.

"Referral Templates hit P0, not the handoff list"

Compliance sign-off was a known dependency before we reached the last sprint. I flagged it at alignment, not at kickoff. The blocker was external. The late flag was mine.

The illustration question

When Pallavi delivered the illustration, I wasn't sure it communicated "$120 total" across all three referral message variants. I flagged it before testing it.

"I kinda liked it, lets roll with it. We can of course change later depending on the performance."
Joe — Design Director

I deferred. Right call for a product context. Ship, then measure. What I'd change: run a quick reading-order test before asking. I raised a question I could have answered myself.

Outcome

Where it landed

Hi-fi signed off by Adithya. Aligned with Ashish and Tanishq. Waiting on dev sprint allocation.

Referral Templates are P0 as of Aug 2026, blocked on compliance availability and Growth team leave. Not dropped. In queue.

No conversion data yet. The hi-fi hasn't shipped.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

  • A lightweight PLU test in November 2025 would have closed the eight-month gap. I didn't push for it. That's the one I'd take back.
  • The Phoenix constraint felt like a limitation. It wasn't. Designing modular from the start — message, visual, CTA as independent variables — produces something Growth can actually run experiments on. I'd apply that constraint deliberately next time.
  • The illustration question deserved a prototype, not a Slack message.
