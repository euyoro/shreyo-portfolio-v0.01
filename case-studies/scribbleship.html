Context

Can we deliver stationery and prints on demand?

In Ahmedabad, ordering personalized stationery or print materials is still an inconvenient, fragmented process. Students, businesses, and academic institutions routinely need custom prints, but there is no single platform that brings the entire workflow together, from browsing to ordering to delivery.

ScribbleShip was built to address that gap. A mobile app designed to streamline how people discover, customize, and order stationery and print materials. The goal was to make the process feel as effortless as ordering food, bringing personalized stationery and prints on demand to a market that had been underserved.

Design and UX principles were used to devise a comprehensive execution plan, from sector research through to a pilot strategy. I designed the full UI, working from UX research through high-fidelity prototyping.

Approach

From sector research to a working prototype

The project moved through four distinct phases, each building on the previous one.

UX Research

Started with sector selection to identify the right market opportunity, followed by ground study and gap analysis in Ahmedabad. Market research confirmed the demand for on-demand print and stationery services. From there, the unique selling proposition was defined: a single-platform solution for personalized stationery and prints with delivery.

Sector Selection & Gap Analysis

User Research

A user survey was conducted to map real behaviors and pain points. This informed the creation of user personas and the end-to-end user journey. The target audience included design and art students, creative young adults and teenagers, companies requiring bulk prints, and professionals seeking a stationery solution.

User Personas

Wireframing

Wireframes were built to test layout and information architecture. A low-fidelity prototype was used to validate the core navigation and task flows before investing in visual design.

UI Prototyping

Usability testing surfaced friction points in the early flows, which were improved before moving to the visual language. SF Pro Display was chosen as the primary typeface. Bright red was used for attention and key actions, with black and white providing contrast. Icons were rounded and soft to match the friendly, approachable tone of the brand. The final high-fidelity prototype covered the full user journey, and a pilot strategy was drafted for launch.

Outcome

A complete mobile experience

The final deliverable was a high-fidelity interactive prototype covering the full user journey: onboarding, browsing stationery, placing print orders, customizing materials, and tracking delivery. Usability testing validated that the core flows were intuitive and the visual language was well-received by the target audience.

A certification course under Saptarishi Prakash deepened my comprehension of the design process throughout.

Reflection

What I took away

  • UX research before UI prototyping is not optional. The sector selection and gap analysis gave the project a foundation that made every downstream decision faster and more grounded.
  • Usability testing in the wireframe stage caught problems that would have been expensive to fix in high fidelity. Testing early is always worth the time.
  • A strong visual language, SF Pro, bright red for action, soft rounded icons, came from understanding the audience first. Design decisions that follow research feel intentional, not decorative.
  • Working across UX research, UI design, project management, strategic planning, A/B testing, and information architecture in a single project built range that carried into later work.
